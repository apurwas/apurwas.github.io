Growth launch

Face authentication KYC: from a day to half an hour

I launched face authentication as a faster alternative to video KYC for digital savings accounts, then scaled it from 0 to 4.5k incremental accounts a month.

Last step
Verify with your face
✓ Identity verified
Account active
My roleProduct Manager, growth
Stage0 to 1 launch, then scale
MetricsAccounts a month, time to activation
Result0 to 4.5k accounts a month
01Context

Opening a digital savings account needs a reliable identity check. The existing route used video KYC (VKYC), followed by a concurrent audit step before the account went live.

02Problem

Customers finished the application and then waited. Activation took about a day. Every hour of waiting is a chance for the customer to drift away, and for the teams opening accounts it made conversion slower and less predictable.

The real problemThe slowest part of the journey came after the customer had already done everything we asked.
03Goal and metrics
PrimaryIncremental accounts opened a month through the new route
SpeedTime from application to an active account
GuardrailVerification stays secure while the journey gets faster
04Insight and solution

Insight

If the customer's face can serve as the identity check, the account can activate in one sitting, and the audit step no longer has to run alongside it.

Solution

Face authentication as the smarter alternative to VKYC, with no concurrent audit leg. The journey stays short and fully digital:

  1. Start in the sales app
  2. Open the lead
  3. Choose the product
  4. Customer identification (VCIP)
  5. Complete the customer profile
  6. Request face authentication
Before: VKYC plus concurrent auditAbout a day
After: face authenticationAbout 30 minutes
Illustrative, roughly to scale.
05Launch and adoption

Adoption is a product problem and a communication problem. I launched with an enablement EDM that explained the journey in six steps and framed face authentication as the smarter alternative to VKYC.

The message was deliberately simple: the same account, far faster, and nothing extra to do.

06Results
0 → 4.5k
incremental accounts a month
~1 day → ~30 min
time to activate an account
0
concurrent audit legs holding the customer up
07What I learned
  • Speed is a growth lever, not just a nicer experience.Shortening the wait after the application did more for volume than adding anything before it.
  • Remove steps, do not just speed them up.Taking the concurrent audit leg out of the customer's path was the bigger win.
  • Launch communication is part of the product.A clear, six-step EDM did the enablement work a long manual could not.

01Context

Every month, a large pool of people started opening a digital savings account and went quiet. These dormant leads had already shown intent, which made them the cheapest growth available.

02Problem

About 1.3L+ leads a month sat idle. The interest was there. What was missing was a simple way for a person to pick up a lead, reach the customer, and help them finish.

The real problemIntent existed, but nobody owned the last mile between a stalled application and an open account.
03Solution

Leads flow in through APIs and appear in a lead app for the people who can convert them. The workflow is three steps:

  1. Find: check unconverted leads
  2. Connect: open the lead and view contact details
  3. Convert: send the link and help the customer finish

Design choices

A time-boxed lead window

Leads stay available for a limited time, so follow-up happens while intent is still fresh.

Trade-off. Some leads expire before anyone reaches them.

Five clear actions

Call, send link, support the customer, save for later, or mark not interested with a reason.

Trade-off. Fewer options, so reasons are captured consistently.

04Growth experiments alongside

Funnel fixes

Helper nudges and a single call to action replaced competing choices.

Result. +4% lead conversion and +35% lead volume.

Referral journey

A referral loop brought in high-quality digital accounts.

Result. 100+ accounts, ₹12.5L+ revenue, 2x higher MDAB.

06What I learned
  • Dormant leads are a growth source, not a loss.Re-engaging people who already showed interest is often the cheapest win.
  • Own the last mile.The gain came from giving someone a clear job and a clear next action.
  • Regulated products are built with risk and compliance.Having them in the pod from the start kept shipping fast and safe.

01Problem

Once a team member shares an account-opening link, the trail goes cold. Which leads are in progress? Which links expired? Who said no? Without one place to look, follow-ups were guesswork and expired leads were easy to forget.

The real problemSending the link was tracked. What happened next was not.
02Solution
  • All leads in one view, searchable by name or mobile number
  • Filters by date, product and status
  • Real-time status, so people act on what matters
  • Expandable lead cards, with the option to resend a link
  • A way to re-engage expired leads and manage not-interested customers
03Status model

Each status tells the user what to do next. That was the design rule.

ActiveApplication in progress. Follow up.
CompletedAccount activated. Nothing to do.
ExpiredThe link expired. Resend or re-engage.
Not interestedLink shared, customer did not start. Record the reason.
04How it fits the funnel

The online-to-offline journey brings leads in and sends links. My Leads closes the loop, so every lead has a visible outcome. Together with face authentication, which made activation fast, it covers the path from lead to active account.

05What I learned
  • A status is only useful if it says what to do next.Four statuses, four clear actions.
  • Follow-up is part of conversion.Tracking after the link is sent matters as much as sending it.

ResolveAI: an AI copilot for support agents

It investigates a customer complaint, gathers the evidence, and tells the agent what to do next, without promising anything the evidence cannot back up. Uses synthetic demo data only.

01Problem

A customer writes that a payment failed but the money left their account. It looks simple and is not. The answer depends on the transaction record, the recipient-side status and the applicable policy. Support goes wrong in two opposite ways: the agent guesses and promises a refund that never arrives, or escalates everything and clogs specialist queues.

What ResolveAI aims forEvidence first, a clear next action, and escalation only when the case needs it.
02Solution

Watch it investigate a real test case on synthetic data: a failed payment where the money was debited.

04Product decisions

Statuses that change what the agent does

Each of the three asks for a different action. A status that changes nothing is only a label.

Trade-off. More routing logic to build and test.

Risk and escalation are separate

High risk does not automatically mean escalate. Escalation depends on policy and findings.

Trade-off. The interface must explain two signals.

The AI never invents an outcome

A promised refund that never arrives is worse than a slower answer.

Trade-off. Some answers are more cautious than customers like.

Customer ID only when needed

General policy questions should not require account data. This fix is in progress.

Trade-off. The system must first classify the question.

05The bug that taught me most
SymptomThe live frontend showed a CORS error and never returned a result.
CauseFrontend and backend sit on different hosts, and the API had not allowed the frontend's address.
FixI updated the backend's allowed origins, redeployed and re-ran the case.
LessonWorking locally is not working deployed. Test the real path.
